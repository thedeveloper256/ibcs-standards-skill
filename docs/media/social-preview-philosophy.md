# Evidence Grid

A visual philosophy for a tool that asks to be trusted with reporting decisions.

The canvas behaves like a measuring instrument, not a poster. Space is divided by a quiet grid, but the grid is never decoration: it is the visible trace of alignment, comparison, and proof. Forms sit on the grid with the patience of a technical drawing. Nothing floats. Nothing crowds. Every margin is checked, every baseline is deliberate.

Color carries meaning before it carries mood. Neutral fields hold the structure; a narrow green and a narrow red appear only where a variance has a direction. A single cool blue marks the neutral reference. The palette stays small because a larger palette would imply distinctions the data does not support. The result should feel calibrated rather than styled, the product of painstaking chromatic restraint.

Scale is hierarchical and honest. A small number of large gestures establish the subject; small mono labels do the precise work of rule IDs, periods, and sources. Typography is sparse and exact. The large words are few enough to be read at a glance, and the small words are placed where a reader will look for evidence. Text never explains what the composition can show.

The composition rewards a second look. Repeated marks, thin rules, and measured intervals create a system that can be read at several distances: the headline first, the evidence second, the source last. The work should appear meticulously crafted, as though every interval was adjusted by hand and every alignment checked against a master grid. It is the visual language of a serious instrument, made with the care of a master craftsperson.

The final artifact should feel inevitable rather than decorative. It does not shout for attention; it earns it by making the structure visible. The viewer should sense that the thing was labored over, refined, and stripped back until only the load-bearing marks remained.
